Labour disputes are increasingly relevant in Georgia. MMLAW offers qualified legal support in disputes between employers and employees.

For employers, we help prevent risk by bringing contracts, internal regulations and policies into line with the law.

What an employment lawyer does

An employment lawyer protects the interests of employees or employers: reviews the contract and the grounds for dismissal, negotiates, prepares complaints and represents you in court.

When you need an employment lawyer

  • You were dismissed and consider it unfair
  • Your salary or compensation isn't being paid
  • You face discrimination or harassment at work
  • You're offered a contract whose terms you don't understand
  • You're an employer and want to prevent disputes

What to do if you are dismissed

  1. Ask for the written grounds of your dismissal.
  2. Keep every document — contract, dismissal order, correspondence, salary statements.
  3. Contact a lawyer immediately — appeal deadlines are short.
  4. If needed, challenge the dismissal in court, asking for reinstatement or compensation.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Signing a "voluntary resignation" under pressure
  • Not asking for the written grounds
  • Missing the appeal deadline
  • Working without a written contract

What we handle

  • Recovery of unpaid salary and compensation
  • Discrimination and harassment at work
  • Employment contracts and internal regulations
  • Collective disputes and negotiations
  • Compliance advice for employers

Questions & answers

What can I do if I was dismissed unlawfully?

You can ask your employer for the written grounds of dismissal and challenge it in court, seeking reinstatement or compensation. Deadlines are short (as a rule, 30 calendar days), so contact a lawyer immediately.

Is an oral employment contract valid?

An oral contract is possible, but a contract concluded for more than one month must be in writing.

Do you represent employees or employers?

Both. We help employees restore their rights, and help employers prevent disputes and represent them in court.

Legal basis

  • Labour Code of Georgia
  • Civil Procedure Code of Georgia

Official texts (in Georgian) — Legislative Herald of Georgia, matsne.gov.ge.
