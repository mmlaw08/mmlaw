# Living in a flat for a lump sum (girao) in Georgia

Girao means you give the owner a lump sum, live in the flat rent-free and get the money back at the end. Legally it is a loan secured by a mortgage on the flat. If the contract isn't done properly and registered, getting your money back can be very hard. We check the flat, prepare the contract and protect you in a dispute.

Under Article 286 of the Civil Code, a private lender that isn't a supervised financial institution generally may not take real estate as security for a loan to an individual. Girao is the exception: when the mortgaged home is handed to the lender to live in. From the third mortgage in favour of the same person, the exception no longer applies.
A mortgage exists only once registered in the Public Registry, and a mortgage securing a loan must be notarised — the notary explains the consequences (Article 289). If a flat has several mortgages, the one registered first has priority (Article 290).

## Before you pay

- Get a fresh Public Registry extract: who owns the flat and whether there is a mortgage, seizure or other restriction.

- Check whether the owner is on the debtors register.

- If the flat has several owners, all of them must sign.

- Sign before a notary and register the mortgage in the Public Registry — before paying or on the same day.

- Pay by bank transfer so the payment is documented.

## What the contract should say

- The amount, currency and exact repayment date

- Your right to live there and who pays utilities and repairs

- What happens if the owner wants to sell or mortgage the flat again

- What happens if the money isn't repaid on time — enforcement through the mortgage

- Early termination terms for both sides

If the owner doesn't repay when the term ends, your mortgage lets you recover the money from the flat through enforcement. The flat can pass into your ownership only on a joint application by owner and creditor (Article 300).

## Questions & answers

### Is girao legal?

Yes — the law expressly allows a home to secure a loan when it is handed to the lender to live in. The key is a notarised contract and a mortgage registered in the registry.

### What if the owner doesn't give my money back?

If the mortgage is registered, you recover the money from the flat through enforcement. Without registration you are an ordinary creditor, in line with everyone else.

### Is a notary required?

Yes. A mortgage securing a loan must be notarised, and it takes effect only when registered in the Public Registry.

### The flat already has a bank mortgage — can I still do girao?

You can, but the bank has priority and gets paid first. We advise against it, or recommend extra protection terms.
