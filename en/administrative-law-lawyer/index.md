Administrative law covers relations with government agencies, licensing, permits and regulatory matters. MMLAW helps you manage bureaucratic processes effectively.
Deadlines in administrative disputes are short, so it matters to contact a lawyer as early as possible — ideally as soon as you receive the decision.

## What an administrative law lawyer does

An administrative law lawyer helps you challenge decisions of public authorities — from fines to permits and tax disputes. We check whether the decision is lawful, prepare the complaint and lawsuit, and represent you in court.

## When you need one

- You were fined and consider it unfair

- A permit, licence or registration was refused

- You have a dispute with the tax or customs authority

- A public body doesn't respond or delays your case

- You were dismissed from the civil service

## Stages of an appeal

- Note the date you received the decision — the deadline runs from that day.

- Administrative complaint to the superior body, where the law requires it.

- Administrative lawsuit in court.

- Appeal to the Court of Appeals and the Supreme Court.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Missing the appeal deadline — it's usually short

- Losing proof of when the decision was delivered

- Ignoring letters from the authority

- Filing a complaint without evidence

## Questions & answers

### What is the deadline to challenge an administrative act in court?

As a rule, one month from the date the act was officially delivered to you. Some cases have different statutory terms, so check the exact deadline immediately.

### Do I have to file an administrative complaint first?

In some cases the law requires a complaint to the superior body before going to court; in others you may go straight to court. We assess which route is faster and stronger.

### Can a fine be appealed?

Yes. A decision on an administrative offence can be appealed to the superior body or to the court within short statutory deadlines.
