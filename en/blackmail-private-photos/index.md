# Blackmail with private photos in Georgia — lawyer

Blackmail with private photos or information is one of the hardest situations — people often tell no one out of shame. The law is strict here: demanding money is extortion, and spreading intimate images violates the secret of private life, with up to 10 years in prison. We act fast and in confidence.

- Courts of all instances

Several crimes can be involved at once: extortion (Article 181) if money or property is demanded; violation of the secret of private life (Article 157¹); spreading personal data (Article 157); secretly recording a private conversation (Article 158).
The victim needs three things: the blackmail to stop, the content removed, and the offender held responsible. We prepare the report, work with investigators, use platforms' takedown tools and claim compensation for moral damage.

## What to do right now

- Don't pay — paying rarely stops blackmail.

- Don't delete the messages; take dated screenshots with the profile link.

- Stop communicating with the offender.

- Contact a lawyer and the police — the sooner, the better.

## Questions & answers

### If I pay, will they stop?

Often not. Paying shows the pressure works, and demands grow. It is better to keep the evidence and contact a lawyer and the police.

### The photo has already been posted — can it be removed?

Yes. We use platforms' takedown tools and, where needed, legal routes — while the criminal case continues.

### Will my name appear anywhere?

We work in complete confidence. There are also ways to protect a victim's private life during the investigation and in court — your lawyer will ask for them.
