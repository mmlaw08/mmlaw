# Inheritance lawyer in Georgia

We help you accept an inheritance in Georgia, obtain the certificate of inheritance and register the property in your name — and represent you in disputes between heirs.

You can inherit under a will or by law. When there is no will, the first-line heirs are the deceased's children, spouse and parents; if there are none, the inheritance passes to the next lines.
An inheritance must be accepted within 6 months of its opening — by applying to a notary or by actually taking possession of the property. The notary then issues a certificate of inheritance, on the basis of which the property is registered in your name with the Public Registry.

## What an inheritance lawyer does

An inheritance lawyer helps you accept the inheritance on time, gather the documents, obtain the certificate of inheritance and register the property in your name. If the heirs can't agree, we represent you in court.

## When you need an inheritance lawyer

- A family member has died and left property

- The heirs can't agree how to divide the property

- You consider the will unfair

- You missed the 6-month deadline

- You live abroad and can't come to Georgia

- The property isn't registered with the Public Registry

## Accepting an inheritance, step by step

- Gather the documents — death certificate, proof of kinship and property documents.

- Within 6 months, apply to a notary to accept the inheritance (or actually take possession of the property).

- The notary issues a certificate of inheritance.

- With the certificate, the property is registered in your name with the Public Registry.

## Documents you'll need

- Death certificate

- Your ID document

- Proof of kinship (birth and marriage certificates)

- Property documents or a Public Registry extract

- The will, if there is one

## Mistakes to avoid

- Missing the 6-month deadline

- Accepting the inheritance without checking the deceased's debts

- Relying on a verbal agreement with the other heirs

- Trying to sell the property before getting the certificate of inheritance

- Putting off old, unregistered property

## Questions & answers

### How long do I have to accept an inheritance?

6 months from the opening of the inheritance — as a rule, the date of death. You can accept it by applying to a notary or by actually taking possession of the property.

### Who are the first-line heirs?

The deceased's children (including adopted children), spouse and parents. They inherit in equal shares unless a will provides otherwise.

### What if I missed the 6-month deadline?

If you missed it for a valid reason, a court can extend it. Whether you actually took possession of the property also matters. We'll assess your case.

### Can a will be challenged?

Yes — for example if the will's form was not respected or the testator was not aware of their actions when making it. Certain close relatives may also be entitled to a compulsory share regardless of the will.

### Am I liable for the deceased's debts?

As a rule, an heir is liable for debts only up to the value of the property inherited. It's worth checking the debts before accepting — if needed, you can renounce the inheritance.
