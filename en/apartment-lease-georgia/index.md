# Renting an apartment in Georgia — the lease

A well-drafted lease protects both sides — the landlord and the tenant. We draft leases in Georgian and English or Russian, check the flat and help in disputes — from unpaid rent to eviction.

If a rented flat is sold after it was handed to the tenant, the new owner takes over the lease (Civil Code, Article 572) — the tenant doesn't have to leave because of the sale. A lease can also be registered in the Public Registry, which makes it visible to buyers and creditors — especially useful for long or commercial leases.
An individual renting out housing for living purposes, without claiming expenses, pays 5% on the rental income (Tax Code, Article 81).

## What the lease should say

- The parties and exact details of the flat — with its cadastral code

- Rent, currency, payment date and method

- Term and renewal

- Advance payment and deposit — when and on what terms it is returned

- Utilities and minor repairs — who pays

- Pets, subletting, registering at the address

- Early termination and notice period

- A condition report and photos at handover

## When registration is worth it

Registration needs a written contract with certified signatures, or both parties signing at the registry. For long or commercial leases we recommend it — it makes your right visible to everyone.

## Questions & answers

### Does a lease need a notary?

No. But to register the lease, signatures must be certified or both parties must sign at the registry.

### The flat was sold — do I have to move out?

No. If the flat was already handed to you, the new owner takes over the lease.

### What tax does the landlord pay?

An individual renting out housing for living purposes without claiming expenses pays 5% of the rent.

### Can the lease be in English or Russian?

Yes. We usually prepare a bilingual text — Georgian plus English or Russian — for the registry and courts.
