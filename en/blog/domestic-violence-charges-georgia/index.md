# Domestic violence charges in Georgia: what to know

Domestic violence under Georgian law: who counts as a family member, the penalties, what makes a charge more serious, and what to do as accused or victim.
[Violent crimes](https://mmlaw.ge/en/violent-crime-lawyer/) · October 6, 2026 · 4 min read · Updated: October 7, 2026

Domestic violence is punished under Article 126¹ of the Criminal Code. Many other crimes against a family member are also treated as "family crimes".

## Who counts as a family member

By law this includes not only a spouse and children, but parents, siblings, grandparents, grandchildren, the spouse's parents, a partner in an unregistered marriage, and a former spouse.

## What counts as domestic violence

Violence, systematic insults, blackmail or humiliation by one family member against another that caused physical pain or suffering.

## Penalties

- Community service of 80–150 hours or imprisonment of up to 2 years

- Against a minor, a pregnant woman or a person with a disability, in front of a minor, against two or more people, by a group or repeatedly — 1 to 3 years

- In both cases the court may also restrict weapons-related rights

## If you are accused

- Don't contact the victim — it can aggravate the charge or become a reason for detention

- Comply with any restraining or protective order

- Give no statement without a lawyer

## If you are a victim

Call 112. The police can issue a restraining order against the abuser. A lawyer can help you apply for a protective order and protect your interests in the criminal case.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who counts as a family member?

A spouse and children, and also parents, siblings, grandparents, grandchildren, the spouse's parents, a partner in an unregistered marriage, and a former spouse.

### What is the penalty for domestic violence?

Community service of 80–150 hours or imprisonment of up to 2 years; with aggravating circumstances, 1 to 3 years. The court may also restrict weapons-related rights.

### I'm accused. Can I contact the victim?

Don't — it can aggravate the charge or become a reason for detention. Comply with any order and give no statement without a lawyer.
