Corruption and official crimes are in Chapter XXXIX of the Criminal Code. These are serious charges: taking a bribe carries at least 6 years and often a ban from holding office.
Evidence in these cases is often obtained through covert investigative actions — secret recordings, controlled handovers. So whether it was obtained lawfully is decisive for the defence.

## Cases we handle

- Taking a bribe (Article 338): 6 to 9 years; a large amount (over GEL 10,000) — 7 to 11 years; an especially large amount (over GEL 30,000) or by extortion — 11 to 15 years

- Giving a bribe (Article 339): up to 3 years; to obtain an unlawful act — 4 to 7 years

- Trading in influence (Article 339¹)

- Abuse of official power (Article 332) and exceeding it (Article 333)

## Release of the bribe-giver

By law, a bribe-giver is released from criminal liability if they voluntarily report it to the authority conducting criminal proceedings. That authority makes the decision — so this step must be planned with a lawyer.

## Questions & answers

### I was asked for a bribe. What should I do?

Don't pay, and contact a lawyer immediately. A bribe-giver is released from liability if they report it voluntarily — this step must be taken correctly.

### What is the penalty for taking a bribe?

6 to 9 years' imprisonment; for a large amount (over GEL 10,000) 7 to 11 years; for an especially large amount (over GEL 30,000), repeatedly or by extortion, 11 to 15 years.

### Who counts as an official?

Public servants and persons treated as such, including employees of public-law legal entities who exercise public powers.

### I was secretly recorded. Is that evidence?

Only if it was obtained in the way the law requires, with the necessary authorisation. Checking this is one of the main tasks of the defence.
