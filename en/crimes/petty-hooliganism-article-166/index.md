# Petty hooliganism in Georgia — Article 166 (admin.)

Petty hooliganism is swearing in a public place, insultingly accosting people and similar acts that disturb public order or people's peace (Article 166 of the Code of Administrative Offences). A similar act committed with violence or the threat of violence is criminal hooliganism (Article 239).

## What matters for the defence

- Whether the place was public and public order was disturbed

- Video and witnesses

- Accuracy of the record — which words and actions were written down

- A personal conflict or a breach of public order

## If you were detained for an administrative offence

- You must be brought before a court within 24 hours; to gather evidence this can be extended once by another 24 hours — otherwise you must be released

- Ask for a lawyer — a court decides the case and the outcome can be administrative detention

- Sign the record only after reading it; if you disagree, write down your objection

- The court's decision can be appealed within 10 days of receiving it

## Questions & answers

### What is the penalty under Article 166?

A fine of GEL 500–3,000 or up to 20 days of administrative detention. On a repeat — GEL 3,000–5,000 or 5 to 60 days.

### How is it different from criminal hooliganism?

Criminal hooliganism (Article 239) involves violence or the threat of violence. Without it, it is usually petty hooliganism.

### Can I appeal?

Yes. The court's decision can be appealed within 10 days of receiving it.
