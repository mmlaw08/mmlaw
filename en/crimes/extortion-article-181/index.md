# Extortion in Georgia — Article 181: penalties

Extortion under Article 181 is demanding someone else's property, a property right or a financial benefit, combined with a threat — of violence, destroying or damaging property, or spreading discrediting or other harmful information. The threat can be aimed at the victim or a close relative.

## What matters for the defence

- Whether the demand was lawful — for example, recovering a real debt

- What the threat said and how real it was

- Messages and recordings, and whether they were lawfully obtained

- Lawfulness of covert investigative actions

## If you are accused or arrested

- Use your right to remain silent and ask for a lawyer — from the first contact with the investigating authority

- Don't sign any document you don't understand

- You must be charged within 48 hours of arrest, and within the next 24 hours a court decides on a preventive measure

- Decide on compensating the damage or settling with the victim together with your lawyer

## If you are a victim

- Call 112 or go to the police or the prosecutor's office

- Keep the evidence: photos, video, messages, receipts, witnesses' contacts

- Ask for victim status — it gives you rights in the case, for example to be informed about its progress

- You can claim compensation for the damage — a lawyer will help

## Questions & answers

### Is demanding a debt extortion?

Not by itself. But demanding repayment with threats of violence or other threats can be treated as extortion or another offence. It is safer to claim a debt through the courts.

### Someone threatens to post my photos unless I pay — what should I do?

Don't pay and don't delete the messages. Keep the evidence and go to the police — this is extortion.

### What is the penalty for extortion?

For the basic offence — a fine or 2 to 4 years in prison; with aggravating circumstances — up to 9 years.

## Crimes and penalties

- [Theft](https://mmlaw.ge/en/crimes/theft-article-177/) — Article 177 Secretly taking someone else's property. Penalties range from a fine to 10 years, depending on the damage and circumstances.

- [Robbery (open theft)](https://mmlaw.ge/en/crimes/robbery-article-178/) — Article 178 Openly taking someone else's property. Penalties range from a fine to 11 years, depending on the damage, violence and other factors.

- [Violent robbery](https://mmlaw.ge/en/crimes/violent-robbery-article-179/) — Article 179 An attack to take property, with violence dangerous to life or health or the threat of it. Penalty: 5 to 15 years.

- [Embezzlement](https://mmlaw.ge/en/crimes/embezzlement-article-182/) — Article 182 Unlawfully keeping or spending property or money you were entrusted with — often at work. Up to 11 years.

- [Damage to property](https://mmlaw.ge/en/crimes/property-damage-article-187/) — Article 187 Damaging or destroying someone else's property causing significant damage. Penalties from a fine to 12 years.

- [Bodily harm](https://mmlaw.ge/en/crimes/bodily-harm/) — Article 117, 118, 120 Intentional minor, less serious and serious bodily harm — penalties from a fine to 14 years.

- [Hooliganism](https://mmlaw.ge/en/crimes/hooliganism-article-239/) — Article 239 A gross breach of public order with violence or the threat of violence. Penalties from a fine to 7 years.
