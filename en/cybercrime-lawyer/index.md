Cybercrime covers unauthorised access to computer systems, unlawful use or damage of data, and the use of tools designed for such acts. Traditional crimes — fraud, extortion, threats and invasion of privacy — are also increasingly committed online.
In these cases, digital evidence decides everything — phones, computers, accounts and transactions. That's why a lawyer should be involved as early as possible: for victims, to preserve the evidence; for the accused, to check whether the digital evidence was obtained lawfully.

## When you need a cybercrime lawyer

- You were defrauded online and lost money

- Your account, email or social media was hacked

- You're being threatened or blackmailed with private photos or information

- Someone created a fake profile or acts in your name

- You've been summoned for questioning in a cybercrime case

- Your phone or computer was seized and you don't know what happens next

## If you are a victim

- Don't delete anything — save messages, links, transactions and dated screenshots.

- Contact your bank immediately if money was taken or you shared card details.

- Change your passwords and turn on two-factor authentication.

- If you're being blackmailed, don't pay and stop communicating with the offender.

- Contact a lawyer — they'll prepare the complaint to the police and protect your interests throughout.

## If you are accused

- Don't give statements without a lawyer — you have the right to remain silent

- Talk to your lawyer before giving out device passwords

- Your lawyer checks whether the digital evidence was obtained lawfully

- If needed, the defense brings in an independent expert

## Mistakes to avoid

- Deleting messages and evidence

- Paying a blackmailer

- Trusting unknown "money recovery" services

- Sharing case details on social media

- Signing a device seizure record without reading it

## Questions & answers

### What counts as cybercrime?

Unauthorised access to a computer system, unlawful use or damage of data, and using tools designed for such acts. Fraud, extortion and threats committed online are criminal offences too.

### I was scammed online. Can I get my money back?

Sometimes, yes — especially if you act fast: contact your bank, keep all the evidence and talk to a lawyer. Don't trust services that promise to recover money for an upfront fee.

### I'm being blackmailed with private photos. What should I do?

Don't pay and stop replying, but don't delete anything. Contact a lawyer right away — blackmail and sharing private images are criminal offences.

### Can the police take my phone?

As a rule, seizing a device and examining its contents requires a court order. Talk to your lawyer before signing any record or giving out passwords.
