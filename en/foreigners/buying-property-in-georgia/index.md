# Buying property in Georgia as a foreigner

Foreigners can buy apartments, houses and non-agricultural land in Georgia on the same terms as Georgians. We check the property, prepare the contract and register your ownership — even if you are abroad.

The purchase itself is quick: ownership arises once it is registered with the Public Registry. The risks are elsewhere — a mortgage or seizure on the property, a seller without full authority, or a developer of a new build without the right permits.
If you can't come to Georgia, you can buy through a notarised power of attorney. Depending on the country, documents issued abroad may need an apostille or consular legalisation (documents from some countries need neither) and a certified Georgian translation — we tell you exactly what to prepare.

## Questions & answers

### Can foreigners buy property in Georgia?

Yes. Foreign individuals and companies can buy apartments, houses, commercial premises and non-agricultural land. Agricultural land is restricted for foreigners, with limited exceptions set by law.

### Does buying property give me a residence permit?

Owning property can be a basis for a short-term residence permit if its value meets the threshold set by law (currently USD 100,000). The permit is not automatic — you have to apply for it.

### Can I buy property without coming to Georgia?

Yes. You can give a notarised power of attorney (with an apostille if the country requires it) to your lawyer or a trusted person, who signs and registers the purchase for you.
